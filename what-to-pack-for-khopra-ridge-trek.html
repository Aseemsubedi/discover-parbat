What to Pack for the Khopra Ridge Trek

A season-by-season packing list for spring, monsoon, autumn and winter, from the local team that walks this ridge all year.

Before you pack

Khopra Danda sits at 3,667 m (12,031 ft). On the way up you walk through warm terraced villages and rhododendron forest, then out onto an open ridge that can freeze overnight in any season except the monsoon.

The trek itself runs seven to nine days. Our Khopra Ridge Trek page has the full 8-day itinerary, route and cost, so this guide sticks to one job: what goes in your bag. First the gear you need whatever the month, then a separate packing list for each season, and at the end a checklist you can tick off on screen or print.

The short version

All year you need broken-in boots, a layering system, rain protection, a head torch, a water bottle and trekking poles. Spring and autumn add a down jacket and a 3-season sleeping bag. The monsoon is about staying dry. Winter needs one more warm layer than Kokhe Danda, a heavier down jacket, a winter sleeping bag and crampons or microspikes after snow.

Conditions on the ridge can be very different from Pokhara. Check the forecast a few days before you leave, and ask us if you are unsure.

Year-round essentials

These items come with you whatever the month. The season sections further down only change how warm the layers are and what you add on top.

Backpack

With a porter, carry a 20 to 30 litre daypack and put the rest in a duffel. Carrying everything yourself, 40 to 50 litres is enough for a lodge-to-lodge trek. Look for padded hip straps and a rain cover.

Resist packing "just in case" clothes. You feel every extra kilo on the long climb up to the ridge.

Layering system

Temperatures swing hard between the lower villages and the exposed ridge. A few thin layers you can add and remove do far more than one bulky jacket. Spring and autumn need four layers. Winter on Khopra needs five.

Base layers and pants

Two or three wicking T-shirts, one thermal top and one pair of thermal leggings cover spring and autumn. Add two pairs of quick-dry trekking pants. Leave jeans and thick cotton at home: they get heavy and stay wet.

Down jacket and shell

The down jacket is the item you will reach for most: evenings in the lodge, early starts, and standing on the ridge for sunrise over Dhaulagiri and Annapurna South. The windproof shell matters because the ridge is open, and wind makes a sunny morning feel far colder than the thermometer says.

Boots, socks and camp shoes

Waterproof boots with good grip and ankle support, already broken in. The trail has long stone staircases, forest sections that stay muddy, and a ridge that can be frozen in the morning.

Three or four pairs of wool or synthetic socks, and flip-flops or light sandals for the lodge.

Never start the trek in brand new boots. Walk at least a few days in them at home first.

Hands and head

Gloves, a beanie that covers your ears, and a sun hat or cap. Sunrise on the ridge is the coldest hour of the day, and that is when you will be standing still with your hands out holding a camera.

Rain, sun and poles

A rain jacket or poncho and a pack cover in every season. Sunglasses with UV protection, sunscreen (SPF 30 to 50+) and lip balm, because UV is strong at altitude and stronger again on snow. Trekking poles are optional, but they save your knees on the long descent from the ridge and help on mud, ice and stone steps.

Water, light and power

A 1 litre bottle and purification tablets or a filter. You can refill along the trail, which saves money and plastic. A head torch with spare batteries for sunrise starts and night trips to the toilet. A 10,000 mAh power bank, since charging at the lodges is limited and sometimes paid.

First aid and toiletries

A small kit with blister plasters, bandages, antiseptic, pain relief, oral rehydration salts and your own prescription medicines. Toothbrush, toothpaste, toilet paper, hand sanitizer, wet wipes and a small quick-dry towel.

Altitude medicine is your doctor's call. Speak to a healthcare provider before your trip about altitude sickness and medicines. Our guides carry a pulse oximeter and a basic first-aid kit and check in with you each morning, but they will not prescribe medicine.

Khopra Ridge vs Kokhe Danda: what changes

If you have already read our Kokhe Danda packing list, most of it carries over. Both are lodge and homestay treks from Pokhara with the same basic kit. The difference is height and exposure. Khopra Ridge is about 360 m higher than the top of the Kokhe Danda Trek (3,309 m), it is more exposed to wind, and you spend more nights high up.

From spring to autumn that mostly means a warmer sleeping bag. In winter it means one full extra layer.

Season Kokhe DandaTop at 3,309 m Khopra RidgeRidge at 3,667 m
Spring Base layer, fleece, light down, shell. Sleeping bag optional from April. Same four layers with a warmer down jacket. 3-season sleeping bag recommended.
Monsoon Rain gear, quick-dry clothes, fleece for evenings. Same, plus a down jacket for the ridge, which stays cool even in July.
Autumn Base layer, fleece, down jacket, shell. Same four layers. 3-season sleeping bag recommended. Winter kit from late November.
Winter Four layers: thermal base, fleece, down jacket, shell. Sleeping bag recommended. Five layers: add an extra mid-layer, a heavier down jacket, a second thermal set, liner plus insulated gloves, a winter-rated bag and crampons or microspikes after snow.

Why one more layer? On a clear winter night, Haljure on the Kokhe Danda trail drops to around -5 to -6°C. Khopra Ridge is higher, open to the wind and colder still, especially in January. The extra mid-layer is what lets you stand outside at sunrise without shivering.

Pack by season

Pick the months you are trekking. Each season below has what the ridge is like, how many warm layers you need up top, the packing list and month-by-month notes.

Spring · Mar–May Monsoon · Jun–Aug Autumn · Sep–Nov Winter · Dec–Feb

SpringMarch to May

Layers on the ridge

Rhododendron season. The forest below the ridge turns red and pink, the days are mild and the lodges are lively. Afternoon cloud and short showers are common, and the ridge still freezes on clear nights, especially in March.

Pack

  • 2 to 3 wicking T-shirts
  • 1 thermal top and 1 thermal legging
  • 1 fleece
  • 1 warm down jacket
  • 1 windproof shell
  • 2 quick-dry trekking pants
  • 3 to 4 pairs of socks
  • Warm gloves and beanie
  • Sun hat and sunglasses
  • Rain jacket or poncho, pack cover
  • Trekking poles
  • 3-season sleeping bag

Month by month

March: still close to winter on the ridge. Bring the winter extra mid-layer if you feel the cold, and expect old snow in shaded spots early in the month.

April: peak bloom and the easiest month to pack for. Four layers are enough.

May: warm and hazy in the valleys, with pre-monsoon showers. Lighten your base layers but keep the rain jacket handy.

Compared with Kokhe Danda: same clothing. Pack a warmer down jacket and a proper sleeping bag rather than relying on blankets.

MonsoonJune to August

Layers on the ridge

The wettest and quietest months. Expect daily rain, slippery stone steps, leeches in the lower forest and mountains hidden in cloud for much of the day. Clear mornings do happen, and the green hillsides are beautiful, but it is the hardest season to trek. Landslides can disrupt roads and trails.

Pack

  • Waterproof jacket and poncho
  • Pack cover and dry bags inside
  • 3 quick-dry T-shirts
  • 2 quick-dry trekking pants
  • 1 fleece
  • 1 light down jacket
  • 5 to 6 pairs of socks
  • Waterproof boots with good grip
  • Sandals for lodges and wet crossings
  • Leech socks or salt, insect repellent
  • Trekking poles
  • Light sleeping bag or liner

Month by month

June: the monsoon arrives in the first half of the month. Early June can still give dry mornings.

July and August: peak rain. Dry bags matter more than any jacket, because a wet sleeping bag or down jacket is useless.

Compared with Kokhe Danda: almost identical. Keep a light down jacket for the ridge, which stays cool even when the valleys are humid.

AutumnSeptember to November

Layers on the ridge

The most popular season, with the clearest views of Dhaulagiri, Annapurna South and Nilgiri. Days on the ridge run at roughly 2 to 7°C, and nights drop below freezing from October onwards. The air is dry and the sun is strong.

Pack

  • 2 wicking T-shirts
  • 1 thermal top and 1 thermal legging
  • 1 fleece
  • 1 warm down jacket
  • 1 windproof shell
  • 2 quick-dry trekking pants
  • 3 to 4 pairs of socks
  • Warm gloves and beanie
  • Sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm
  • Light rain jacket
  • Trekking poles
  • 3-season sleeping bag

Month by month

September: the monsoon tails off during the month, so keep full rain gear for early September and expect some muddy sections.

October: the classic month. Four layers, and the down jacket comes out every evening.

November: colder, clearer and quieter. Add thicker socks and warmer gloves. From late November, pack as for winter.

Compared with Kokhe Danda: same layers, but treat the sleeping bag as essential rather than optional.

WinterDecember to February

Layers on the ridge

Crisp, quiet and often the clearest skies of the year, with the fewest trekkers. It is also properly cold: nights on the ridge fall well below freezing and can reach around -10°C or lower in cold spells, with January usually the coldest month. In recent winters snow has stayed above about 2,700 m, so the lower trail is usually clear, but the ridge itself can hold snow and ice for days after a storm.

Pack

  • 2 thermal tops
  • 2 thermal leggings
  • 2 wicking T-shirts
  • 1 warm fleece
  • 1 extra mid-layer (second fleece or light synthetic jacket)
  • 1 heavy down jacket, warmer than for autumn
  • 1 windproof shell
  • 2 trekking pants
  • Liner gloves plus insulated gloves
  • Warm beanie and a neck gaiter or buff
  • 3 to 4 pairs of warm socks, plus a thick pair for sleeping
  • Waterproof boots
  • Trekking poles
  • Winter sleeping bag, about -10 to -15°C

Also bring

  • Crampons or microspikes after snowfall
  • Gaiters if fresh snow is forecast
  • Insulated bottle, since water can freeze overnight
  • Sleeping bag liner for extra warmth

Month by month

December: cold and very clear. Full winter kit on the ridge, though the lower villages are pleasant by day.

January: the coldest month and the most likely time for snow. Bring traction and don't skip the extra mid-layer.

February: days start to warm but snow can still fall. Pack as for January and check conditions before you leave.

Compared with Kokhe Danda: one more warm layer, a heavier down jacket, a second thermal set and traction for snow. Your guide will tell you before you leave Pokhara whether crampons are needed for your dates.

Do you need a sleeping bag?

On Kokhe Danda a sleeping bag is optional for most of the year. On Khopra Ridge we recommend one in every season except the height of the monsoon. The lodges have warm dining rooms where everyone gathers in the evening, but the bedrooms are unheated and the ridge is cold after dark.

  • Spring and autumn: a 3-season bag rated to about -5 to -10°C, depending on how much you feel the cold.
  • Monsoon: a light bag or a liner is usually enough.
  • Winter: a bag rated to about -10 to -15°C, plus a liner and a thick pair of socks to sleep in.

You don't need to buy one. Sleeping bags and down jackets are easy to rent in Lakeside, Pokhara, the day before the trek.

Packing for the Khayer Lake day

Many trekkers add the side trip from Khopra Ridge to Khayer Lake, the sacred lake at 4,653 m. That is almost 1,000 m above the ridge, and it is a long day out and back.

  • Wear or carry every warm layer you have, including the down jacket.
  • Carry water and snacks for the whole day. The small restaurant at Khayer Khola closes in winter.
  • Crampons or microspikes in winter and after any snowfall.
  • Sunglasses and sunscreen, since snow reflects a lot of UV.
  • Head torch, in case the return takes longer than planned.

What to leave at home

Packing light matters as much as packing right. For Khopra Ridge you do not need:

  • Jeans or heavy cotton sweaters
  • More than one pair of boots
  • Several bulky jackets instead of layers
  • A big towel or full-size toiletries
  • Lots of bottled water or snacks from home
  • Laptops and other electronics you won't use

Build one layering system that works for your season, and stop there.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a sleeping bag for the Khopra Ridge Trek?

Yes, for most of the year. Rooms at Khopra Ridge (3,667 m) are unheated and nights drop below freezing from autumn to spring. A 3-season bag rated to about -5 to -10°C suits spring and autumn. In winter, bring a bag rated to about -10 to -15°C. Monsoon nights are milder, but a light bag or liner is still worth carrying.

Do I need crampons for Khopra Ridge in winter?

After snowfall, yes. Snow and ice on the upper trail and the ridge make crampons or microspikes necessary. On dry winter days you may not use them, so your guide will tell you before you leave Pokhara whether to bring them.

How is packing for Khopra Ridge different from Kokhe Danda?

In spring, monsoon and autumn the clothing is almost the same, but Khopra needs a warmer sleeping bag. In winter Khopra needs one more warm layer than Kokhe Danda: an extra mid-layer between your fleece and down jacket, a heavier down jacket, a second thermal set and traction for snow.

What extra should I carry for the Khayer Lake hike?

Khayer Lake sits at 4,653 m, about 1,000 m above Khopra Ridge. Wear every warm layer you have, carry enough water and snacks for the whole day, and bring crampons or microspikes in winter. The small restaurant at Khayer Khola closes in winter.

Can I buy or rent trekking gear in Pokhara?

Yes. Lakeside in Pokhara has many shops that sell and rent down jackets, sleeping bags, poles and microspikes, so you can fill gaps before the trek. Bring your own broken-in boots.

Final tips

Khopra Ridge does not need an expedition kit. Pack a good layering system for your season, a sleeping bag that matches the nights, and in winter that one extra layer. Everything else is comfort.
