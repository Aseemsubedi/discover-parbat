About Mardi Himal and Poon Hill Trek

The Mardi Himal and Poon Hill Trek brings two of the Annapurna region's finest walks together in a single 10-day journey. You begin on the high forested ridge of Mardi Himal, climb to the Upper Viewpoint and Mardi Base Camp, then cross to Ghandruk, Tadapani and Ghorepani for the famous sunrise at Poon Hill. From there, instead of turning back the usual way, the trail continues through Kokhe Danda and down into the village trails of Parbat.

The Mardi Himal section is a narrow, open ridge that rises steadily above the rhododendron and oak forest. After a drive from Pokhara to Kande, you climb through Australian Base Camp to Suire Danda, then on to Low Camp and High Camp at 3,553m. Above the treeline, Machhapuchhre (Fishtail), Annapurna South, Hiunchuli and Mardi Himal stand almost within reach, and the ridge is far quieter than the more crowded routes in the region.

The highlight of this section is the early morning hike from High Camp to the Upper Viewpoint and Mardi Base Camp. Starting before dawn, you climb for two to three hours to watch the first light touch the peaks, with a wide sweep of the Annapurna range behind you. It is a demanding morning, but the reward is one of the closest mountain panoramas you can reach without technical climbing.

After descending to Rest Camp and Landruk, the trek moves into Gurung country. Ghandruk is one of the largest Gurung villages in Nepal, with stone-paved lanes, traditional houses and close views of Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre. A restful morning here gives your legs a break before you continue to Tadapani through Bhaisikharka, and then to Ghorepani through rhododendron and oak forest via Banthati and Deurali.

Poon Hill (3,210m) is the second highlight. A short, steep climb before breakfast puts you on one of the best sunrise viewpoints in Nepal, with Dhaulagiri and the Annapurna range turning gold in the first light. From here the route leaves the standard circuit and follows the ridge to Kokhe Danda (3,309m), a quiet viewpoint that most trekkers never visit.

The final two days show a side of the region that few visitors see. You descend through Jaljala to Lespar, then walk down via Haljure to Banau, traditional villages in the Parbat district where life still follows the rhythm of farming and the seasons. From Banau, a private vehicle takes you back to Pokhara in about two and a half hours.

The best seasons are spring (March–May), when rhododendrons bloom along the ridge and forest trails, and autumn (October–November), when skies are at their clearest. Winter is possible for well-prepared trekkers but the nights at High Camp are very cold. In every season, we recommend keeping to the itinerary's gradual pace to allow time to acclimatise.

Day by Day

Detailed Itinerary

We start with a morning drive from Pokhara to Kande, about 34 KM and an hour away. From Kande we begin our trek through the forest, passing Australian Base Camp, a lovely viewpoint on the way. From here the trail climbs steadily to Suire Danda, where we stay overnight.

Altitude: 2,438m Distance: 7.3 KM Time: 4 Hrs (+1 Hr drive) Stay: Tea House

Today we continue up the ridge through thick rhododendron and oak forest to Forest Camp, where we stop for lunch. The trail keeps climbing gently through the trees, and views open up whenever the forest thins. We reach Low Camp in the afternoon and spend the night here.

Altitude: 3,004m Distance: 9.6 KM Time: 6–6.5 Hrs Stay: Tea House

This is a shorter but steady uphill day. We climb via Badal Danda, where the forest gives way to open ridge and the Himalayan views become much closer. We reach High Camp early enough to rest and adjust to the altitude before the big day tomorrow. With clear weather, the sunset and evening views of Machhapuchhre and the Annapurna range are superb.

Altitude: 3,553m Distance: 5 KM Time: 3.5–4 Hrs Stay: Tea House

This is the main day of the trek. We leave very early in the morning for the Upper Viewpoint and Mardi Base Camp, a two to three hour climb along the narrow ridge. If the sky is clear, we reach the top in time to watch the sunrise over the Annapurna range.

After spending time at the top, we return to High Camp for breakfast and then start the long descent. We follow the ridge down via Badal Danda and continue to Rest Camp, where we stay overnight. Expect around four hours of downhill walking after the climb, so this is a long day on the legs.

Altitude: 2,600m (Rest Camp) Distance: 12.5 KM Time: 2–3 Hrs up, 4 Hrs down Stay: Tea House

We leave the ridge behind and descend for about three hours to Landruk, a Gurung village beside the Modi Khola. After a break here, we cross the river and climb for around two hours to Ghandruk, one of the biggest and most beautiful Gurung villages in Nepal. We spend the night here.

Altitude: 2,000m Distance: 8.2 KM Time: 3 Hrs down + 2 Hrs up Stay: Tea House

We enjoy a restful morning in Ghandruk with early views of Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre, and time to wander through the village lanes. Later, we trek uphill via Bhaisikharka to Tadapani, a walk of around three and a half hours through forest. Tadapani sits on a ridge with a close view of Annapurna South and Hiunchuli.

Altitude: 2,705m Distance: 6.2 KM Time: 3.5 Hrs Stay: Tea House

A day of mixed uphill and downhill walking through beautiful rhododendron and oak forest. We drop to Banthati for lunch, then climb steadily to Deurali, where the trail reaches its highest point of the day at around 3,100m, before the final stretch into Ghorepani. It is about six hours of walking in total.

Altitude: 2,880m Distance: 8.4 KM Time: 6 Hrs Stay: Tea House

We start the morning with an hour of steep climbing to Poon Hill (3,210m), one of the most celebrated sunrise viewpoints in Nepal. In clear weather, Dhaulagiri, Annapurna and Machhapuchhre glow in the first light. After returning to Ghorepani for breakfast, we leave the busy trail and follow the ridge for about three hours to Kokhe Danda, a quiet viewpoint with wide Himalayan views.

Altitude: 3,309m Distance: 6 KM Time: 1 Hr up, 3 Hrs to Kokhe Danda Stay: Tea House

After sunrise views from Kokhe Danda, we begin a long downhill day of about five and a half hours. The trail passes through Jaljala, where the snow peaks remain in view, and continues down through forest and farmland to the village of Lespar, where we stay overnight.

Distance: 9.5 KM Time: 5.5 Hrs

We trek down via Haljure to Banau, a walk of around three hours, where our vehicle is waiting. From Banau it is a two and a half hour drive back to Pokhara.

Our 10 Days Trek ends here.

Altitude: 2,100m (Banau) Distance: 7.1 KM Time: 3 Hrs hike + 2.5 Hrs drive

Along the Mardi Himal ridge and at Ghorepani and Tadapani you stay in simple tea houses with twin rooms and shared bathrooms. At higher camps facilities are basic and rooms are unheated. On the final night in the Parbat village of Lespar, accommodation is simple and village-style, with home-cooked food.

Sturdy trekking boots, a sleeping bag rated to around -10°C, a down jacket, a waterproof shell, warm base layers, trekking poles, a headlamp, a reusable water bottle with purification tablets, sunscreen, sunglasses and a comfortable daypack. We send a full packing list after booking.
