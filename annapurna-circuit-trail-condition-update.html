Annapurna Circuit Trek Update: Trail, Road & Flood Conditions

Jeeps reach Chame, the Chame bridge is still under water, and trekkers are crossing Thorong La again. Here is the full section-by-section picture.

The latest in brief

The recent storm and heavy rainfall caused flooding, landslides and damage to several roads and bridges along the Annapurna Circuit. The situation is improving.

Road repairs are underway, trekking is possible along most of the circuit, and trekkers have already started crossing Thorong La Pass again. Below is the current picture around Chame, the alternative route to Talekhu, access to Manang, Thorong La, and your options for getting back to Pokhara.

Flood and road conditions after the storm

Heavy rainfall from the recent storm caused flooding and multiple landslides along the lower and middle sections of the circuit. Road crews have started clearing and repairing the damaged sections.

As of the latest update, jeeps can now reach Chame, a significant improvement on the conditions immediately after the storm. The situation at Chame itself, however, remains complicated.

Chame bridge still submerged

The bridge at Chame is still submerged under the Marsyangdi River. It was the main crossing linking the road and trail sections beyond Chame, so this matters. Excavation and river-clearing work is underway to reopen the channel and eventually the road.

The damaged bridge does not completely cut off the higher sections of the circuit for trekkers.

The alternative route from Chame to Talekhu

Trekkers can currently take an alternative route from Chame toward Talekhu and rejoin the regular Annapurna Circuit trail there. Once at Talekhu, you continue on the normal trail toward the higher sections.

This is not a regular trekking trail. Some sections are steep, exposed and sketchy, and in places you will need to hold onto rocks and bushes for balance. It can be tricky for inexperienced trekkers or anyone carrying a heavy pack.
  • Take extra care and allow additional time.
  • Travel with a local guide or someone who knows the area.
  • Keep your pack as light as possible for this stretch.

Road blocked between Talekhu and Bhratang

Beyond this section, the road is still blocked in several places toward Bhratang. Multiple landslides and damaged sections mean jeeps cannot run continuously through this stretch.

Trekking through the area is possible. Expect landslide debris and damaged road, and plan for slower progress than usual.

Jeeps operating between Bhratang and Manang

The situation improves further ahead. Jeeps are operating between Bhratang and Manang, so you can walk or save time with a jeep where it suits you. From here onward, conditions are considerably more normal.

Manang to Thorong La

The trail from Manang toward Thorong La is in normal trekking condition overall. Trekkers have crossed Thorong La for the past two consecutive days.

Recent sunny weather is reducing the snow around the pass, which makes the crossing easier than it was just after the storm. More trekkers are expected to cross in the coming days.

Still a high pass. Thorong La is a high-altitude crossing and conditions can change quickly. Check the latest weather and trail conditions before attempting it, and keep a spare day in your itinerary.

What happens after Thorong La?

The western side of the circuit is also improving, but road damage still affects transport. You have three main options, depending on your time and budget.

Option 1 · Fastest

Fly from Jomsom

One of the quickest ways back toward Pokhara. Flights depend on weather and airport conditions, so keep some flexibility.

Option 2

Trek to Galeswor

Walk down the Kali Gandaki valley to Galeswor, then take road transport toward Pokhara and beyond.

Option 3 · Most walking

Via Ghorepani to Nayapul

For those with extra days. Finish the western Annapurna on foot through the lower hills and Ghorepani.

Road conditions between Beni and Ghasa

The road between Beni and Ghasa is one of the main remaining transport problems on the western side. Repair and reconstruction are still underway after the storm damage.

The work is expected to take at least another week, possibly longer, depending on weather, river levels, landslides and crew progress. Don't rely on a fixed road schedule through this section, and confirm conditions locally before you travel.

Is the Annapurna Circuit Trek possible right now?

Yes, the trek is currently possible, but don't expect completely normal conditions throughout. The key points:

  • Jeeps can reach Chame.
  • The Chame bridge is submerged under the Marsyangdi River.
  • An alternative route from Chame to Talekhu bypasses the bridge, but it is not a regular trail and has tricky, exposed sections.
  • The road is blocked in several places toward Bhratang, but trekking through is possible.
  • Jeeps run between Bhratang and Manang.
  • The trail from Manang to Thorong La is generally normal, with crossings on two consecutive days and snow decreasing.
  • After the pass: fly from Jomsom, trek to Galeswor, or continue via Ghorepani to Nayapul.
  • The Beni–Ghasa road is under repair for at least another week.

Should you cancel your Annapurna Circuit Trek?

There is no need to cancel automatically because of the storm damage. However, treat the circuit as an evolving route rather than a completely normal itinerary for now.

The biggest challenges are around Chame and the damaged road toward Bhratang. Be prepared to walk around damaged sections, deal with landslide debris and use the temporary route around Chame. The good news is that the route beyond Manang is in much better shape, and recent Thorong La crossings show the high section is opening up as snow decreases.

Get a local update right before you depart, and stay flexible with transport and accommodation plans.

Final note

The Annapurna Circuit remains one of Nepal's most spectacular treks, and conditions are improving as repair and clearing work continues. Some sections may take longer and involve more walking than usual, but with preparation, local information and flexibility, the circuit is accessible.

Discover Parbat will keep monitoring the situation and update this article as road, bridge and trail conditions change.
