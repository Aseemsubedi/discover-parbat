Overview

About Everest View Trek

The Everest View Trek is a 7-day, moderate trek into the heart of the Khumbu, following the first part of the Everest Base Camp trail from Lukla to Namche Bazaar and Tengboche Monastery. On the way it climbs to Hotel Everest View at 3,880 m, where you can sit on the terrace with a cup of tea and look straight up the valley to Everest, Lhotse, Nuptse and Ama Dablam. It is the shortest way to see Everest up close on foot, without the two weeks and the high altitude of the full base camp trek.

The trek starts with one of the most famous flights in the world, a short hop into the steep mountain airstrip at Lukla. From there the trail follows the Dudh Koshi river through pine forest and Sherpa villages, crossing high suspension bridges before the long climb to Namche Bazaar, the main town of the Khumbu, with its bakeries, shops and stone lodges stacked around a mountain bowl.

Above Namche, the itinerary goes over the ridge to Hotel Everest View and then down to Khumjung, a large Sherpa village below the sacred peak of Khumbila, where Sir Edmund Hillary founded the village school in 1961. Climbing to the hotel and sleeping a little lower in Khumjung also helps your body adjust to the altitude before Tengboche.

The trek's other high point is Tengboche, where the Khumbu's most important monastery sits on a forested ridge with Everest and Ama Dablam behind it. Evenings in the teahouses here, warming up by the stove with trekkers heading to and from base camp, are part of what makes the Khumbu special. We then walk back down through Namche to Lukla for the flight home.

This is a busy trail in spring and autumn, and it depends on mountain flights at both ends. If you would rather avoid the Lukla flight and the crowds, see our Pikey Peak vs Everest View Trek comparison, or our road-access Pikey Peak Trek, which gives a wide sunrise view of Everest from the lower Solukhumbu.

Plan Your Trek

Best Time, Flights and Permits

Best time: October and November give the clearest views of Everest after the monsoon, and March to May brings rhododendron blooms to the forest below Tengboche. Winter (December to February) is cold but often clear and much quieter.

Lukla flights and Ramechhap: in the busy seasons, roughly October to November and March to May, most Lukla flights leave from Manthali Airport in Ramechhap rather than Kathmandu. That means an early pickup at around 1 to 2 am and a 4 to 5 hour drive before the flight, and the same drive back after you land on the last day. Outside those months we usually fly directly from Kathmandu. The airlines set the schedule each season, and we confirm your departure airport before you start.

Permits: the trek enters Sagarmatha National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, so you need the national park permit and the Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality entry fee. We arrange both.

Altitude: you sleep no higher than Tengboche (3,867 m), and the route climbs to Hotel Everest View before sleeping lower in Khumjung, which helps you acclimatise. Read our guide on how to avoid altitude sickness before you go.

⚠ Keep 2 to 3 spare days for Lukla flights

Flights in and out of Lukla only fly in good visibility, and cloud or wind can cancel them, sometimes for several days in a row. If your flight home is booked for the day after the trek, a delay can make you miss it. Please plan 2 to 3 spare days in Kathmandu between Day 7 and your flight home.

If the flights run on time, you can use the extra days to explore Kathmandu, Bhaktapur or Patan. If they don't, you won't be under pressure. Extra hotel nights and any helicopter you choose to take are not included in the trek price, so travel insurance that covers delays and helicopter evacuation is strongly recommended.

Teahouses in peak season: lodges in Namche and Tengboche fill up in October and April. We book ahead for our groups; if you are planning independently, read should you book teahouses in advance in Nepal.

Day by Day

Detailed Itinerary

The day starts with the flight to Lukla. In the busy trekking seasons (roughly October to November and March to May) most Lukla flights leave from Manthali Airport in Ramechhap, not Kathmandu. On those dates we pick you up from your hotel at around 1 to 2 am for the 4 to 5 hour drive to Ramechhap, then take the short flight of about 20 minutes to Lukla. Outside those months we usually fly straight from Kathmandu, a flight of about 35 minutes. We confirm the exact airport and pickup time a few days before you start, because the airlines set it.

Landing at Tenzing-Hillary Airport in Lukla (2,860 m), on a short sloping runway cut into the mountainside, is one of the most memorable moments of the trip. After tea and a short break while our porter organises the bags, we start walking. The trail descends gently along the Dudh Koshi valley, past Sherpa villages, mani stones and prayer wheels, and reaches Phakding (2,610 m) in about 3.5 to 4 hours. The first day is deliberately easy, so you can get used to the trail and the altitude.

Altitude: 2,610m Distance: 7.8 KM Time: 3.5–4 Hrs trek Stay: Tea House

We follow the Dudh Koshi upstream, crossing it several times on suspension bridges, through Benkar and on to Monjo, where we enter Sagarmatha National Park and our permits are checked. After lunch at Jorsalle, the trail crosses the high Hillary Suspension Bridge and then climbs steeply for around two hours to Namche Bazaar. On a clear day you get your first view of Everest from a bend partway up the climb.

Take this climb slowly. You gain roughly 800 m today, and walking at an easy, steady pace is the best thing you can do for acclimatisation. Namche, the main Sherpa town of the Khumbu, sits in a horseshoe-shaped bowl below Kongde Ri, with bakeries, cafés, gear shops and some of the most comfortable lodges in the Everest region.

Altitude: 3,440m Distance: 11 KM Time: 6–7 Hrs Stay: Tea House

This is the day the trek is named after. From Namche we climb steeply above the town to the Syangboche airstrip and on to Hotel Everest View, at 3,880 m the highest point of the trek. Built in 1971 by a Japanese hotelier, it once held the Guinness record as the highest-placed hotel in the world. From its terrace, over a cup of tea, you look straight up the valley to Everest, Lhotse and Nuptse, with Ama Dablam, Thamserku and Kangtega close by.

From the hotel we walk down to Khumjung (3,780 m), one of the largest Sherpa villages in the Khumbu, with green-roofed houses spread across a wide valley below the sacred peak of Khumbila. Here you can visit the Khumjung school, founded by Sir Edmund Hillary in 1961, and the village monastery. The walking day is short, which gives your body time to adjust to the height: going up to 3,880 m and sleeping a little lower is a good way to acclimatise.

Altitude: 3,880m Distance: 4 KM Time: 3 Hrs Stay: Tea House

We leave Khumjung and join the main Everest Base Camp trail, which traverses high above the Dudh Koshi with Ama Dablam ahead of us for much of the morning. The trail then drops steeply to Phunki Tenga, by the river at about 3,250 m, where we stop for lunch.

From the river it is a steady climb of around 600 m through pine and rhododendron forest to Tengboche (3,867 m). Tengboche Monastery is the largest and most important monastery in the Khumbu, and if you arrive in time you may be able to sit in on the afternoon prayers. As the sun goes down, the light on Everest, Lhotse, Nuptse and Ama Dablam from the monastery grounds is one of the finest sights of the trek.

Altitude: 3,867m Distance: 6.5 KM Time: 5 Hrs Stay: Tea House

If the morning is clear, step outside early for sunrise over Everest and Ama Dablam before breakfast. We then walk back down through the forest to Phunki Tenga (about 3,300 m), cross the river and climb back up to the main trail. The final stretch is a long, scenic traverse above the valley, with views back towards Ama Dablam and Tengboche, before the path drops slightly into Namche. The afternoon is free for the cafés, a last browse in the shops, or simply a rest.

Altitude: 3,440m Distance: 9.4 KM Time: 6–7 Hrs Stay: Tea House

This is the longest walking day of the trek, but most of it is downhill. We drop steeply from Namche to the Hillary Suspension Bridge, then follow the Dudh Koshi back through Jorsalle, Monjo and Phakding. The last hour or so is a gentle climb back up to Lukla. In the evening we celebrate the end of the trek with our team and reconfirm the next morning's flight.

Altitude: 2,860m Distance: 18.8 KM Time: 7–8 Hrs Stay: Tea House

Flights leave Lukla early in the morning, when the weather is usually at its best. Outside the busy seasons we fly straight to Kathmandu. In the busy seasons we fly to Ramechhap and drive back to Kathmandu in 4 to 5 hours, arriving in the afternoon. This is the day most likely to be affected by weather, which is why we strongly recommend keeping 2 to 3 spare days before your flight home (see below).

Time: Flight (+ 4–5 Hrs drive from Ramechhap in peak season)
Route Map

Everest View Trek Route Map

The 7-day Everest View Trek starts with a flight to Lukla (2,860 m), from Kathmandu or, in the busy seasons, from Ramechhap. You walk down the Dudh Koshi valley to Phakding, then via Benkar, Monjo and Jorsalle across the Hillary Suspension Bridge and up to Namche Bazaar (3,440 m). From Namche you climb past the Syangboche airstrip to Hotel Everest View (3,880 m), the highest point of the trek, and sleep in Khumjung. The trail then drops to Phunki Tenga and climbs to Tengboche Monastery (3,867 m), facing Everest, Lhotse, Nuptse and Ama Dablam, before you return via Namche to Lukla for the flight back.
