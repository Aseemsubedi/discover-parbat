Mardi Himal to Khopra Danda: How the Route Connects via Ghandruk, Tadapani and Dobato

The trail that links two of the Annapurna region's best ridges without going back to Pokhara: every leg from the Mardi Himal viewpoint to the Khopra community lodge, with altitudes, walking times and the turns people miss.

The short answerCan you walk from Mardi Himal to Khopra Danda?

Quick answer

Yes, on foot and without returning to Pokhara. From the Mardi Himal ridge you descend through Landruk to the Modi Khola, climb to Ghandruk, then follow forest trails through Tadapani and Dobato to the Muldai Viewpoint and Khopra Danda. After the Mardi Himal viewpoint day, it takes four more walking days, about 32 km, to reach the Khopra community lodge.

Mardi Himal and Khopra Danda sit on opposite sides of the Modi Khola valley, so most trekkers treat them as two separate trips. They don't have to be.

A line of old village and forest trails joins them: down off the Mardi ridge, across the river, up through the Gurung village of Ghandruk, and into the quiet rhododendron forest that leads to Dobato and the open ridge at Khopra. It is the backbone of our Mardi Himal & Khopra Ridge (Danda) Trek, and our guides walk it every season.

This guide explains how the connection works, leg by leg: how far each section is, how much you climb and drop, what the trail is like, and where people take the wrong turn. For the full day-by-day itinerary, prices and departures, see the trek page itself.

The connection breaks naturally into four walking days. The figures below come from our own guiding records and are typical for a steady group.

Counting days: the first row is the morning you climb to the Mardi Himal viewpoint and descend, so the connection itself is four days of walking after that: to Ghandruk, Tadapani, Dobato and Khopra.

Altitude profileDown to the river, then back up to the ridge

The shape of this route surprises people. You drop more than 2,500 m off the Mardi ridge in a day and a half, then spend the next three days climbing back up to a higher ridge on the other side of the valley, with one more big dip, to about 2,800 m, between Dobato and Khopra.

That profile is why this direction works well for the Khayer Lake day hike above Khopra. You have already slept several nights above 3,000 m on the Mardi side, so your body has had time to adjust before you climb towards 4,600 m.

Leg by legWhat each section of the trail is like

1. Off the Mardi ridge: High Camp to Rest Camp

  • About 15 km
  • About 7 hrs
  • 4,000 m → 2,600 m

The day starts around 4:30 am with the climb from High Camp to the Mardi Himal Lower Viewpoint at about 4,000 m, where Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre feel close enough to touch. After the views you retrace the open ridge to High Camp and keep descending past Badal Danda into forest, down to Rest Camp.

It is a long day with a lot of downhill. Trekking poles help, and it is worth keeping something warm handy for the cold, windy ridge in the early morning.

2. Across the valley: Landruk, the Modi Khola and Ghandruk

  • About 8.6 km
  • About 6 hrs
  • Down to 1,300 m, up to 1,940 m

This is the leg that actually crosses from the Mardi side to the Khopra side. You descend through Landruk all the way to the Modi Khola at around 1,300 m, the lowest point of the whole route, and cross the river. After about 30 minutes of climbing from the valley floor you reach the road towards Ghandruk, a good place for lunch.

The afternoon is the hardest climb of the connection: two to two and a half hours of steep stone steps up to Ghandruk. Take it slowly. The reward is one of the most attractive Gurung villages in Nepal, with stone houses, flagstone lanes and a close view of Annapurna South and Hiunchuli.

Want to skip the climb? A road runs up from the valley to Ghandruk, and in the main seasons plenty of jeeps go up it. From the lunch stop by the road, you can ask for a seat in a shared jeep and ride up instead of walking the stone steps.

3. Into the forest: Ghandruk to Tadapani

  • About 6.7 km
  • About 5 hrs
  • 1,940 m → 2,630 m

Spend the morning in Ghandruk walking through the older, traditional part of the village before leaving. The trail then climbs steadily through Bhaisikharka into oak and rhododendron forest to Tadapani, a small clearing with lodges and, on a clear morning, a sunrise view of Annapurna South, Hiunchuli and Machhapuchhre.

4. Leaving the main trail: Tadapani to Dobato

  • About 6.2 km
  • 5 to 6 hrs
  • 2,630 m → about 3,500 m

At Tadapani, most trekkers turn west towards Ghorepani and Poon Hill. You don't. The route to Dobato heads up through Isharu into dense rhododendron forest that sees a fraction of the traffic, climbing almost 900 m to the small settlement of Dobato.

This is the quietest and most beautiful section of the connection, especially in spring when the rhododendrons bloom, and also the one where it pays to know the way.

Where to stay in Dobato: we highly recommend Hotel Viewtop, run by a lovely local couple. It is where our groups stay, and the welcome, the food and the warm dining room after a day in the forest make it one of the best nights on the route.

5. Onto the ridge: Dobato, Muldai Viewpoint and Khopra Danda

  • About 11 km
  • About 7 hrs
  • Down to about 2,800 m, up to 3,660 m
  • Hardest day of the connection

Wake early for the 25-minute walk from Dobato to the Muldai Viewpoint. At sunrise it shows Dhaulagiri, the Annapurnas and, on clear days, peaks towards Manaslu, one of the widest panoramas in the region. Then it is back to the lodge for breakfast and the toughest day of the connection.

From Dobato at about 3,500 m, the trail drops steeply through forest to around 2,800 m, then climbs all the way back up through Chhistibung to Khopra Danda at about 3,660 m. That is roughly 700 m down and 850 m up in one day, so pace yourself, carry snacks and water, and don't let the short distance on the map fool you.

Khopra is an open alpine ridge with a community lodge looking straight across the Kali Gandaki gorge at Dhaulagiri. If you have the time and energy, the next day's hike to the sacred Khayer Lake is the high point of the whole trip.

PlanningMardi first or Khopra first?

You can walk the connection in either direction, but we recommend starting with Mardi Himal for three reasons.

  • Better acclimatisation. Mardi takes you above 3,500 m early. By the time you reach Khopra and the Khayer Lake hike, you are already adjusted.
  • The big climb comes on fresher legs. The steep stone steps from the Modi Khola to Ghandruk are easier on day five than at the end of a two-week trek.
  • A natural finish. From Khopra the route continues downhill to Swanta and Ghorepani for Poon Hill, so you end on classic viewpoints and an easy exit.

Going the other way, from Khopra to Mardi, works too, but you would climb out of the river to Landruk and finish with the steep descent off the Mardi ridge to Kande or Siding.

Practical tipsMistakes to avoid on the connection

  • Following the Ghorepani signs at Tadapani. The main trail and most signs point towards Ghorepani. The way to Dobato climbs off to the north through the forest; ask at your lodge before setting out.
  • Underestimating the Ghandruk climb. After a day and a half of descent, two hours of steep steps feel harder than the numbers suggest. Start early and carry water, or take a shared jeep up from the road in the main seasons.
  • Treating Dobato to Khopra as a short day. It is only about 11 km, but you drop to around 2,800 m and climb back to 3,660 m. Leave early after the Muldai sunrise.
  • Turning up at Dobato or Khopra without a booking in peak season. Both have only a few lodges. In October, November and spring, book ahead or trek with a team that books for you. Our guide to booking teahouses in Nepal explains how.
  • Skipping the Muldai sunrise. It is only 25 minutes from Dobato and one of the best viewpoints on the whole route.
  • Ignoring winter conditions. From December to February, snow can cover the forest above Tadapani and the ridge at Khopra. Check conditions and lodge openings before you go.

Honest adviceDo you need a guide for this route?

The sections through Landruk, Ghandruk and Tadapani are well-trodden village trails that experienced trekkers can follow on their own. The Tadapani to Dobato forest and the walk on to Khopra are different: quieter, less signposted and higher, with few people to ask if you are unsure.

A local guide is not enforced on this route, but we recommend one for that stretch. Beyond showing the way, a guide books the small lodges at Dobato and Khopra, adjusts the plan to the weather and explains the Gurung villages and forests you pass through. Our guides are from the region and walk this connection every season.

You need an Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) for the whole route. A TIMS card is not required.

Beyond KhopraWhere the route goes next

From Khopra, the trail descends through Chhistibung to Swanta and climbs to Ghorepani for Poon Hill. From there you have two choices: hike down to Banthati and drive back to Pokhara, or continue along the quiet ridge to Kokhe Danda and a Magar homestay in Lespar. FAQQuestions about the Mardi Himal to Khopra route

Can you trek from Mardi Himal to Khopra Danda without going back to Pokhara?

Yes. From the Mardi Himal ridge you descend through Landruk to the Modi Khola, climb to Ghandruk, and continue through Tadapani and Dobato to the Muldai Viewpoint and Khopra Danda, all on foot.

How long does it take to walk from Mardi Himal to Khopra?

Four walking days after you leave the Mardi ridge: Rest Camp to Ghandruk, Ghandruk to Tadapani, Tadapani to Dobato, and Dobato to Khopra Danda via the Muldai sunrise. Counting the Mardi Himal viewpoint day, it is five days and about 47 km.

What is the highest point between Mardi Himal and Khopra?

Khopra Danda itself, at about 3,660 m. On the way, Dobato sits at about 3,500 m, and the trail dips to around 2,800 m between Dobato and Khopra. The lowest point is the Modi Khola crossing at about 1,300 m.

Is the trail from Tadapani to Dobato easy to follow?

It is a clear forest path, but it is quiet and less signposted than the main trail to Ghorepani, which most signs at Tadapani point towards. Ask at your lodge before leaving, or walk with a local guide.

Can you skip the uphill from the Modi Khola to Ghandruk?

Yes. A road climbs from the valley to Ghandruk, and in the main seasons many jeeps run on it. You can ask for a seat in a shared jeep from the roadside lunch stop instead of walking up the stone steps.

Where should I stay in Dobato?

We highly recommend Hotel Viewtop, run by a lovely local couple. Our groups stay there. Dobato has only a few lodges, so book ahead in peak season.

Can you do the route in reverse, from Khopra to Mardi Himal?

Yes, but we recommend Mardi first. It acclimatises you before Khopra and the Khayer Lake hike, and lets you finish downhill towards Ghorepani and Poon Hill.

Do you need a separate permit for Mardi Himal and Khopra?

No. Both are inside the Annapurna Conservation Area, so one ACAP permit covers the whole route. A TIMS card is not required.
