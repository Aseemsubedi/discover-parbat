Khayer Lake: The Sacred Lake Above Khopra Ridge

A small sacred pond at 4,653 metres below Annapurna South, where pilgrims come to make a wish and return to give thanks. What the lake means to local people, and how to hike there from Khopra.

Khayer Lake is a small, sacred pond tucked below Annapurna South at about 4,653 metres. Few international trekkers have heard of it, yet every year on Janai Purnima, people from the villages below and far beyond climb up to stand at its shore and make a wish.

For trekkers, Khayer Lake is the high point of a long day hike above Khopra Ridge. The trail climbs nearly 1,000 metres from the ridge, past a lone teashop at Khayer Khola and up a final stretch of stone steps, while Dhaulagiri seems to move closer with every hour of walking.

Don't let the word "lake" set your expectations too high. The water itself is a modest pond. What makes the day unforgettable is the trail, the views and the meaning the place holds for the people below.

Most people reach the lake as an extra day on the Khopra Ridge Trek, staying two nights at the community lodge on Khopra and hiking up and back in between. This guide covers what the lake means to local people, what the trail is really like, where you can eat on the way, how to handle the altitude and when to go.

The lakeWhat and where is Khayer Lake?

Khayer Lake, sometimes written Khayer Tal, lies in the Annapurna Conservation Area in Gandaki Province, Nepal. It sits in a high basin beneath Annapurna South, roughly 1,000 metres above the ridge known as Khopra Danda.

There are no lodges at the lake. The nearest overnight stop for trekkers is the community lodge on Khopra Ridge, and the nearest food is the small teashop at Khayer Khola, about halfway up.

It's really a pond, so set your expectations

Despite the name, Khayer Lake is a pond rather than a lake. It measures roughly 150 metres long and 70 metres wide. If you are picturing a vast turquoise lake like those in the Everest or Langtang regions, this isn't that.

The pond itself isn't the reason to come. The reward is the journey: the climb above Khopra, Dhaulagiri growing closer with every hour, Annapurna South towering overhead at the top, and the quiet of a high place that local people hold sacred. Go for the trail and the views, and the pond becomes a fitting place to turn around rather than a disappointment.

Where does the name Khayer Lake come from?

The "lake" in the name may not mean lake at all. One local explanation is that it comes from lek, the Nepali word for high pasture or upland country, as in the common phrase lek-besi, the highlands and the lowland valleys. Over time, as the name was written down for trekkers, "lek" may have become "lake", which helps explain why a modest pond carries such a grand English name.

CultureWhy Khayer Lake is sacred: wishes and Janai Purnima

To people in the hills below, Khayer Lake is far more than a viewpoint. It is believed that what you wish for at the lake will be fulfilled.

When someone makes a wish or asks the lake for something, they take a small piece of it home with them, usually a little mud or a stone from the shore. If the wish comes true, they are expected to return on Janai Purnima to give thanks, bringing back what they took and offering worship at the lake. Traditionally this thanksgiving includes an animal sacrifice, most often a sheep or a goat.

Janai Purnima, the full-moon festival that usually falls in August, is the one day of the year when the lake becomes busy. Pilgrims come from the villages lower down the mountain and from much further away. In the days before the festival they make their way up and stay in temporary shelters or at the lodges and teashop at Khopra and Khayer Khola. On the morning of the festival, they climb to the lake to worship.

For a trekker, seeing the lake through the eyes of the people who hold it sacred changes the experience. It is a place of prayer first and a hiking destination second.

If you visit, treat the lakeshore as you would a temple. Don't throw anything into the water, don't move or take offerings, and ask before photographing pilgrims, especially around Janai Purnima.

Getting thereHow to get to Khayer Lake

Khayer Lake is reached on foot from Khopra Ridge, which is itself a two-day walk from the nearest roadhead. Our route reaches Khopra via Ghandruk, Tadapani, Dobato and the Muldai Viewpoint, arriving at the community lodge on day three.

From Khopra, the lake is a day hike. Most trekkers stay a second night at the community lodge, walk to the lake and back in between, and then continue their trek the following morning. On our Khopra Ridge Trek, Khayer Lake is an optional extra day, which turns the eight-day trek into a nine-day Khopra Ridge and Khayer Lake trek. The hikeThe hike from Khopra Ridge to Khayer Lake

The trail from Khopra to Khayer Lake is about 8.2 km and usually takes four to five hours going up, depending on your pace and how you handle the altitude. The way back down is quicker, but with time at the lake it still adds up to a full day, so start early.

  • Khopra Ridge (3,660 m): Leave the community lodge after an early breakfast and follow the ridge up above the lodge.
  • Khopra to Khayer Khola, 2–2.5 hours: Mostly a gradual uphill along the ridge at first, becoming steeper later on. This is where Dhaulagiri starts to feel noticeably closer.
  • Khayer Khola: A small teashop and cottage beside the stream, the only place to eat and rest on the route.
  • Khayer Khola to Khayer Lake, 1.5–2 hours: About 30 minutes of fairly flat walking, then the trail turns steep on stone steps for the final 1 to 1.5 hours of climbing.
  • Khayer Lake (4,653 m): Rest, take in the views of Annapurna South and the Dhaulagiri range, then retrace the same trail back to Khopra.

How hard is the Khayer Lake hike?

The first half is not technically difficult. The real challenge is the last section above Khayer Khola, where steep stone steps meet thin air above 4,000 metres. Even strong walkers find themselves stopping often here.

The best approach is simply to go slowly. This is a high-altitude zone, and pushing hard on the steps is the quickest way to end up with a headache and a long walk down. If you have walked comfortably to Khopra and feel well there, you are in a good position to try it, as long as you are willing to turn back if your body tells you to.

Food on the trailThe teashop at Khayer Khola

About two to two and a half hours above Khopra, at Khayer Khola, a small teashop and cottage serves trekkers and pilgrims. The menu is short and welcome: dal bhat, chowmein, noodles, soup and hot and cold drinks.

It is a good place for a break on the way up and a meal on the way down. However, it can be closed during the low season, when few people walk this trail. Ask at the Khopra lodge the evening before whether it is open, and carry snacks and water either way.

ViewsMountain views on the way to Khayer Lake

Khopra Ridge is already one of the finest Dhaulagiri viewpoints in Nepal. What surprises most people on the way to Khayer Lake is how much better it gets.

As soon as you leave the ridge and start climbing, Dhaulagiri seems to come closer, and the higher you go, the wider and more dramatic the views become. Across the deep Kali Gandaki gorge, the Dhaulagiri range fills the horizon, and on clear mornings the snow and rock look close enough to touch.

At the lake itself, Annapurna South towers directly above, with the Dhaulagiri range stretching across the other side of the sky. Standing between two of the great massifs of western Nepal is a moment that stays with people long after the trek.

AltitudeAltitude and safety at Khayer Lake

At 4,653 metres, Khayer Lake is about 1,000 metres higher than the Khopra community lodge, and far higher than anywhere else on the Khopra Ridge Trek. You gain that height in a single morning, which is a big jump.

The saving grace is that you go up and come straight back down to sleep at 3,660 metres, which is the safest way to visit high ground. Even so, altitude sickness can develop on the day, so it is worth knowing the signs.

Turn back and descend if you or anyone in your group develops a headache that keeps getting worse, nausea or vomiting, dizziness, unusual tiredness, or breathlessness even when resting. Descending is the most effective treatment, and the lake will still be there another year.

Arriving at Khopra already well acclimatised helps a great deal. On our route, trekkers spend a night at Dobato at around 3,500 metres and a night at Khopra before attempting the lake, which gives the body time to adjust.

Best timeBest time to visit Khayer Lake

Mid-October to mid-December is the best time for most trekkers. After the monsoon the air is clear, the mountains stand out sharply and the trail is dry. By December the mornings are cold and nights on the ridge drop close to freezing, so warm layers matter.

March to mid-May is the other good window. Spring brings longer days and rhododendrons in bloom in the forests on the way up to Khopra, although afternoon cloud can build up and hide the peaks.

The monsoon, June to September, has its own beauty. The high slopes turn green and wildflowers appear around the trail. But the mountains are often hidden in cloud, trails are wet, and the main trek route is harder going. This season does include Janai Purnima, so pilgrims make the climb whatever the weather.

Mid-December to February is winter on the high trail. It is very cold above 4,000 metres, and snow can make the steep final section difficult or unsafe. Check conditions locally before going.

For the best chance of clear views, aim for mid-October to mid-December or March to mid-May.

Permits & guidesPermits and guides for Khayer Lake

Khayer Lake lies within the Annapurna Conservation Area, so you need an Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) and a TIMS card, the same as for the Khopra Ridge Trek. Both are included when you trek with us.

Foreign trekkers in the conservation area must also walk with a licensed guide. On this trail a guide earns their place: the route above Khayer Khola is far less travelled than the main trekking corridors, and a local guide will know when the weather or the altitude means it is time to turn around.

Practical tipsPractical tips for the Khayer Lake hike

  • Start early: Leave Khopra soon after first light. Clouds often build by late morning, and you want time to walk the steep section slowly.
  • Ask about the teashop: Check at the Khopra lodge the evening before whether the Khayer Khola teashop is open.
  • Carry food and water: Take snacks, at least a litre or two of water and a way to treat refills, even if the teashop is open.
  • Pack warm layers: It is much colder and windier at the lake than at Khopra. Bring a warm jacket, hat, gloves and a waterproof shell. See our Khopra Ridge Trek packing list.
  • Protect yourself from the sun: Sunglasses, sunscreen and a hat are essential at 4,600 metres.
  • Walk slowly on the stone steps: The last hour or so is steep and high. Steady, small steps work better than bursts of speed.
  • Know when to turn back: Set a turnaround time, and descend if anyone shows signs of altitude sickness.
  • Respect the lake: It is a sacred site. Leave offerings untouched and carry all your rubbish back down.

Is Khayer Lake worth it?

Yes, as long as you go for the journey rather than the pond. If you are already at Khopra and feeling strong, the hike to Khayer Lake is one of the most rewarding days you can spend in the Annapurna region. It takes you higher than almost any short trek in the area, with Dhaulagiri and Annapurna South closer than most trekkers ever see them.

More than that, it brings you to a place that still matters deeply to the people who live below it. The pieces of mud and stone carried home in hope, and the pilgrims who climb back up to give thanks, are a reminder that these mountains are not only scenery. For many trekkers, that is what makes the long climb worthwhile.

FAQFrequently asked questions about Khayer Lake

Where is Khayer Lake?

Khayer Lake is a sacred high-altitude lake in the Annapurna region of Gandaki Province, Nepal. It sits below Annapurna South, above Khopra Ridge on the Khopra Ridge Trek.

What is the altitude of Khayer Lake?

Khayer Lake sits at about 4,653 metres above sea level, roughly 1,000 metres higher than the community lodge on Khopra Ridge at 3,660 metres.

How big is Khayer Lake?

Khayer Lake is really a pond, about 150 metres long and 70 metres wide. The name may come from lek, the Nepali word for high pasture, rather than the English word lake. Most trekkers go for the trail and mountain views rather than the pond itself.

How long does it take to hike from Khopra Ridge to Khayer Lake?

The trail from Khopra Ridge to Khayer Lake is about 8.2 km and usually takes 4 to 5 hours uphill. Most trekkers return to Khopra the same day, so plan for a full day on the trail.

Is there food on the way to Khayer Lake?

Yes, in season. A small teashop at Khayer Khola, about 2 to 2.5 hours from Khopra, serves dal bhat, chowmein, noodles, soup and drinks. It can be closed in the low season, so ask at the Khopra lodge before setting out and carry snacks either way.

Is the Khayer Lake hike difficult?

It is a demanding day. The first half is mostly gradual, but the final hour or so climbs steeply on stone steps above 4,000 metres. Fit trekkers who are already acclimatised at Khopra can manage it if they walk slowly and turn back if they feel unwell.

Can I get altitude sickness going to Khayer Lake?

Yes. You gain around 1,000 metres in a single morning, so altitude sickness is a real risk. Walk slowly, drink water, and descend if you develop a worsening headache, nausea, dizziness or breathlessness at rest.

Why is Khayer Lake sacred?

Local people believe that wishes made at Khayer Lake are fulfilled. Every year on Janai Purnima, pilgrims from nearby villages and further away come to worship at the lake, including people returning to give thanks after a wish has come true.

When is the best time to visit Khayer Lake?

Mid-October to mid-December and March to mid-May are the best times, with the clearest mountain views. The monsoon brings green slopes and wildflowers, but clouds often hide the mountains.

Can I visit Khayer Lake on the Khopra Ridge Trek?

Yes. Khayer Lake is an optional extra day on the Khopra Ridge Trek. You spend a second night at the Khopra community lodge and hike to the lake and back in between, which makes the trek nine days.

Do I need a guide for Khayer Lake?
