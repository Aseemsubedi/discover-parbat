Pikey Peak vs Everest View Trek: Which Short Everest Trek Should You Choose?

Two week-long treks, two very different ways to see Everest. One starts with the Lukla flight and walks into the Khumbu. The other drives in, needs no permits and looks at Everest from a quiet ridge far to the south.

Short answer: it depends on how fixed your dates are

If your days are fixed and you want a quiet, cultural trek, choose Pikey Peak. If you want to fly into Lukla and walk through Namche to Tengboche, and you have a spare day or two in case flights are cancelled, choose the Everest View Trek.

Pikey Peak4,065 m · road access · no permits · quiet
Everest View TrekLukla flight · Namche & Tengboche · busier
Everest viewPikey: wide and distant · Everest View: close-up

Both treks take about a week and both promise a sunrise over Everest, which is why people end up deciding between them. They feel very different on the ground. The Everest View Trek follows the start of the Everest Base Camp trail, from Lukla through Namche Bazaar to Tengboche. Pikey Peak (4,065 m) sits in the lower Solukhumbu, far to the south, and is reached by road without the Lukla flight.

We run both. Our Pikey Peak Trek is a 7-day trip from Kathmandu, and our Everest View Trek goes as far as Tengboche and returns the same way. Here is an honest comparison, including where the Everest View Trek is the better pick.

The view: a wide panorama or a close-up

This is the biggest difference between the two, and it is worth being clear about it.

From Pikey Peak: half the Nepal Himalaya in one line

From the summit of Pikey Peak, on a clear morning, the horizon runs from the Annapurnas in the west to the Kanchenjunga range in the east. Annapurna I, Manaslu, the Langtang range, Shishapangma, Gaurishankar, Everest, Lhotse, Makalu and Kanchenjunga are all in the same sweep. Pikey is said to have been Sir Edmund Hillary's favourite view of Everest.

It is a panorama, though, not a close-up. The nearest big peaks are Numbur and Karyolung. Everest is far away and shows behind the ridge of another mountain, so it looks smaller than in photos from the Khumbu. You come to Pikey for the length of the view, not for the size of Everest.

You don't have to wait for the summit. Dhap, where the trek starts after the drive from Kathmandu, already looks out to Everest and Lhotse in clear weather.

Sunrise on Pikey is at its best on the short days from November to late March. The summit is about an hour's walk above Pikey Base Camp, an hour and a half at most depending on your pace, so it is an easy pre-dawn start.

From the Everest View Trek: Everest up close

On the Everest View Trek you walk into the Khumbu itself. From the Everest View Hotel above Namche, and again at Tengboche, Everest, Lhotse, Nuptse and Ama Dablam are much closer and fill more of the sky. If seeing Everest big and near is what matters most, the Everest View Trek wins.

Our verdictPikey Peak gives the wider view, Everest View the closer one. Choose by which matters more to you.

Getting there: the Lukla flight vs the road

For many trekkers this decides it.

The Everest View Trek starts with the flight to Lukla, one of the great experiences of the trip. In the busy spring and autumn seasons most Lukla flights leave from Ramechhap (Manthali) rather than Kathmandu, which means a drive of several hours before dawn to reach the airport. The bigger issue is weather. Lukla flights can be cancelled for days in a row, and if that happens at the end of your trek, a helicopter is the only way out. If your international flight home is fixed, that is a real risk.

Pikey Peak is reached entirely by road. The drive from Kathmandu to Dhap takes about 10 hours, and the drive back from Salleri about 12. Most of the road is paved, with a rough two-to-three-hour off-road section along the Roshi Khola riverbank. It is a long day in a jeep, but it doesn't get cancelled because of clouds over a mountain airstrip.

Be ready for some road walking on Pikey. From Dhap to Boudha, where you stop for lunch on the second walking day, the trail mostly follows a road with very little traffic, using shortcuts where they exist. The last two hours into Junbesi and most of the walk to Salleri are also on the road. The real trail is the section from Boudha over Pikey Peak.

Our verdictThe Lukla flight is an adventure in itself, but it needs spare days. With fixed dates, Pikey's road access is the safer choice.

Crowds and culture

The Everest View Trek uses the same trail as Everest Base Camp as far as Tengboche, so in spring and autumn you share it with a steady stream of trekkers, porters and yak trains. Namche Bazaar is lively and full of cafés, and the monastery at Tengboche is one of the most important in the Khumbu.

Pikey Peak is still very quiet compared with the Annapurna treks or the upper Everest region. We have walked it twice, in late December and early June, both in the low season, and met only a few groups on the whole trek. In peak season you should expect a few more people, but nothing like the crowds on Poon Hill or Mardi Himal.

The culture is the other half of Pikey. You pass through Sherpa villages with chortens, mani walls and prayer flags. You spend two nights in Junbesi, a Sherpa village in a wide green valley, and take a side hike to Thupten Chholing, a large Tibetan Buddhist monastery that is home to a community of monks and nuns.

Our verdictFor quiet trails and village life, Pikey. For the buzz of Namche and the famous Khumbu trail, Everest View.

Teahouses

The Everest View Trek has some of the best-developed trekking lodges in Nepal, especially in Namche, with bakeries, cafés and a wide choice of rooms.

On Pikey Peak the teahouses are basic, apart from Junbesi, which has more comfortable lodges. Expect simple rooms and a short menu. Hot showers are available, heated by gas geysers.

Permits and altitude

Permits: the Pikey Peak Trek needs no trekking permits. The Everest View Trek enters Sagarmatha National Park, so you need the national park permit and the local Khumbu rural municipality fee. Both are simple to arrange, but they add a little cost and paperwork.

Altitude: Pikey Peak's summit, 4,065 m, is higher than any point on the Everest View Trek. You only visit it briefly at sunrise before heading back down, though. On the Everest View Trek you sleep higher for longer, in Namche at about 3,440 m and Tengboche at about 3,867 m, so the usual itinerary includes an acclimatisation day in Namche. On both treks, read up on how to avoid altitude sickness before you go.

Neither trek is the "lesser" option. The Everest View Trek is the better choice for a close look at Everest and the famous Khumbu villages. Pikey Peak is the better choice when your dates are fixed, when you want the trail to yourself, and when you care as much about the villages and monasteries as about the mountains. It also gives you a longer stretch of the Himalaya than almost any other short trek in Nepal.

Frequently asked questions

Can you see Everest from Pikey Peak?

Yes. On a clear morning Everest is part of a panorama that runs from the Annapurnas to Kanchenjunga, along with Lhotse, Makalu, Shishapangma, Gaurishankar and the Langtang range. It is a distant view, though: Everest shows behind the ridge of another mountain, and the closest big peaks are Numbur and Karyolung.

Do you need to fly to Lukla for the Pikey Peak Trek?

No. Pikey Peak is reached by road. The drive from Kathmandu to Dhap takes about 10 hours, and the drive back from Salleri about 12 hours, so there is no risk of flights being cancelled by bad weather.

Do you need a permit for the Pikey Peak Trek?

No. The Pikey Peak Trek doesn't need any trekking permits. The Everest View Trek enters Sagarmatha National Park, so it needs the national park permit and the local Khumbu rural municipality fee.

How long is the climb from Pikey Base Camp to the summit?

About an hour, and an hour and a half at most, depending on your pace. Most trekkers leave Pikey Base Camp before dawn to reach the summit for sunrise.

Which is busier, Pikey Peak or the Everest View Trek?

The Everest View Trek. It follows the main trail to Everest Base Camp as far as Tengboche. Pikey Peak is still very quiet. Even in peak season it is less busy than Poon Hill or Mardi Himal.

When is the best time for the Pikey Peak sunrise?

The short days from November to late March give the nicest sunrises from Pikey Peak. October and November usually have the clearest views of Everest and the eastern Himalaya.
