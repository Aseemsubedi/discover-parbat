Manaslu Trek Update: 3 Best Alternatives to Explore in Nepal

Despite claims that the route is practically open again, the Manaslu Circuit is still not safe for ordinary trekking. Here is the latest situation, and three Himalayan treks worth considering instead.

Manaslu is still not safe to trek. Your Himalayan trip doesn't have to wait.

The Manaslu Circuit and Tsum Valley remain cut off from the south after a devastating flash flood destroyed or seriously damaged several bridges, and some agencies are now giving trekkers a misleading picture of the conditions.

As of our latest update on 21 October 2026, the Manaslu Circuit is not safely accessible for ordinary trekking. Local people have built makeshift passages around destroyed sections, but these are not a trekking trail, and we would be very surprised if conditions improved enough within the next week. If you were planning a Himalayan trek in Nepal this autumn, you may need to rethink your route.

The good news: there is no need to give up on Nepal's mountains. From the legendary Thorong La Pass on the Annapurna Circuit to the panoramic ridges of the Multiple Viewpoints Trek and the remote wilderness of Api Himal, there are excellent alternatives for different interests, experience levels and schedules. Below, we explain the current Manaslu situation and introduce three treks worth considering.

Latest update · 21 October 2026Manaslu Circuit and Tsum Valley: the situation now

The Manaslu Circuit Trek and Tsum Valley remain severely affected by the flash flood and the landslides that followed. Several critical river crossings have been destroyed or badly damaged, and there is still no safe, established route through the affected sections from the south.

Not safe for trekking Status as of 21 October 2026
  • Not safely accessible for ordinary trekking from the south, despite claims that it is "practically open"
  • Local people have built makeshift passages around destroyed sections, but these are steep, unstable and not a trekking trail
  • Suspension bridge near Philim (about 90 m) and several other bridges destroyed or damaged
  • No significant improvement expected within the next week
  • Trekkers already inside can only exit north over Larkya La, or by helicopter from Tsum Valley

Manaslu Circuit: please don't be misled New

Some trekking agencies are currently telling clients that the Manaslu Circuit is practically open again. This is misleading and potentially dangerous.

It is true that local people have managed to build makeshift routes around sections of the trail destroyed by flooding and landslides. Their effort is remarkable. But describing these improvised structures as a trekking trail seriously misrepresents the situation on the ground.

What some agencies say

"The Manaslu Circuit is practically open again."

The reality on the ground

Only improvised passages exist around destroyed sections. Some are extremely steep, unstable and difficult to negotiate. The route is not safe for ordinary trekking.

Some of these sections are so steep and unstable that even experienced local Nepalis struggle to get through them. They are not suitable for the average trekker, and certainly not with a full pack, after days of walking, or in poor weather.

Unfortunately, some agencies appear more interested in selling treks than in giving potential clients an accurate picture of conditions. Please do not confuse the existence of a makeshift passage with a safe, established trekking route.

Your safety is more important than any trekking agency's business.

Questions to ask if an agency says Manaslu is open

If you are being offered a Manaslu departure right now, ask these before you pay anything:

  1. How exactly are the destroyed sections and the Philim bridge crossed at the moment?
  2. Has the route been officially confirmed safe by local authorities, or is it only a local makeshift passage?
  3. What happens if a section is too dangerous or collapses again while you are on the trail?
  4. Is helicopter evacuation arranged, and does your insurance actually cover this situation?
  5. Can you postpone or switch to another trek without losing your deposit?

If the answers are vague, treat that as your answer.

What has happened to the Manaslu trekking route?

The flash flood has caused significant damage to trails and infrastructure across the region. Among the most serious losses is the approximately 90-metre suspension bridge near Philim, an essential crossing for anyone travelling through the area. Its damage, along with that of several other bridges and landslide-hit trail sections, makes restoring access far more complicated than routine trail repair.

Repair work continues wherever conditions allow, and local communities have improvised passages to keep people and supplies moving. However, the scale of the destruction means a safe trekking route is unlikely to be restored quickly.

Can trekkers still continue the Manaslu Circuit?

Trekkers who were already north of Nyak Phedi when the disaster struck can continue north without major disruption. Their only safe exit, though, is over Larkya La Pass into the Annapurna region, as the southern route is still not safe for trekking.

For trekkers in Tsum Valley, the alternative exit is a challenging two-day detour across difficult terrain, after which they still need to cross Larkya La to leave the region. The only other option for those affected in Tsum Valley is helicopter evacuation.

These circumstances are a reminder of why it matters to follow local safety advice and check trail conditions before setting out on any remote Himalayan trek.

Is it safe to plan the Manaslu Circuit Trek now?

No. For now, do not attempt to enter the Manaslu Circuit or Tsum Valley from the south. As of 21 October 2026 the route is not safely accessible for ordinary trekking, and we would be very surprised if conditions improved sufficiently within the next week.

If you have an upcoming Manaslu booking, contact your trekking agency, confirm the latest conditions with local authorities, and discuss postponement or an alternative itinerary. Don't assume a trail is safe just because someone says it is "open", because it appears on a trekking map, or because it was open in previous seasons.

Conditions will change as reconstruction progresses, so get a fresh, locally verified update before making any plans.

Alternative 1Annapurna Circuit Trek: the classic Himalayan crossing

Best for: trekkers seeking a high mountain pass, dramatic landscapes, traditional Himalayan villages and a challenging multi-day adventure.

If you chose Manaslu for its high-altitude scenery, remote villages and the feeling of crossing a major Himalayan route, the Annapurna Circuit Trek is one of the strongest alternatives.

This classic journey combines changing landscapes, distinctive mountain cultures and spectacular views of the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges. Discover Parbat's 12-day itinerary starts with a scenic drive from Kathmandu to Chame, then follows the Marsyangdi River valley up towards the highlands of Manang.

What makes the Annapurna Circuit special?

The trail passes through pine forest, dramatic rock formations and traditional settlements before reaching the drier, high-altitude landscapes of Upper Pisang, Ngawal and Manang. As you climb, the scenery changes dramatically, while monasteries, stone houses and Tibetan-influenced traditions add real cultural depth.

Manang is an important acclimatisation stop before the trail continues to Yak Kharka and Thorong High Camp.

The big highlight is the crossing of Thorong La Pass at 5,416 metres, one of the world's best-known high-altitude trekking passes. The demanding climb rewards you with expansive mountain views before the trail descends to Muktinath, a sacred pilgrimage site for both Hindus and Buddhists.

From Muktinath, the route continues through the dramatic Kali Gandaki Valley, with the chance to explore Kagbeni, Jomsom, Marpha and Kalopani. Apple orchards, traditional architecture and views of Dhaulagiri and Nilgiri make for a memorable finish.

Why choose the Annapurna Circuit instead of Manaslu?

The Annapurna Circuit offers a comparable sense of Himalayan scale, a challenging high pass and rich cultural experiences. Road access makes a shorter itinerary possible, although road development has changed some sections of the traditional route. It suits travellers who want a substantial high-altitude trek but don't have time for a longer expedition.

Not a risk-free swap. The Annapurna Circuit isn't automatically safe just because Manaslu is affected. Confirm the latest road, bridge, trail and weather conditions before departure, especially given the recent extreme weather in Nepal.

Alternative 2Multiple Viewpoints Trek: six Himalayan viewpoints in one journey

Best for: trekkers who want panoramic mountain views, varied landscapes, quieter trails and authentic village life, without sticking to one busy trekking corridor.

What if your next Himalayan trek connected six of the region's most rewarding mountain panoramas instead of just one?

The 14-day Multiple Viewpoints Trek by Discover Parbat links Mardi Himal Viewpoint, Muldai Viewpoint, Khopra Ridge, Khayer Lake, Poon Hill and Kokhe Danda. Together they show the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges from constantly changing angles, from dramatic ridgelines and alpine pastures to peaceful hilltops far from the busiest trails. Starting and finishing in Pokhara, it combines well-known highlights with lesser-visited routes and local culture.

From Mardi Himal to the quieter ridges of Khopra

The journey begins with a drive from Pokhara to Kande, then climbs through rhododendron and oak forest towards Mardi Himal. The trail reaches high ridges with close-up views of Machhapuchhre and Annapurna South.

From there, the route drops to the Modi Khola and climbs to Ghandruk, a traditional Gurung village known for its stone houses, narrow lanes and mountain backdrop. Continuing through Tadapani to Dobato, you reach Muldai Viewpoint, with wide-ranging views across the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges.

The trail then heads to Khopra Ridge, where the landscape opens into alpine pasture and broad panoramas. The community lodge on the ridge looks across the Kali Gandaki gorge to Dhaulagiri, with Annapurna South and Nilgiri completing the skyline.

Khayer Lake, Poon Hill and the hidden beauty of Kokhe Danda

One of the most memorable days is the challenging excursion from Khopra to Khayer Lake at about 4,660 metres. Lying beneath Annapurna South, this sacred high-altitude lake adds a remote and spiritual dimension to the trek.

The route then descends to Swanta and climbs to Ghorepani for Poon Hill. Instead of joining the usual pre-dawn rush, our itinerary schedules the visit after breakfast, which can mean a quieter experience depending on conditions and visitor numbers.

Beyond Poon Hill, the trek continues into Parbat district to Kokhe Danda, a lesser-known viewpoint with expansive Himalayan panoramas. This section sets the itinerary apart from conventional Annapurna treks, following quieter trails through Magar villages.

The final stretch passes through Lespar, where a family homestay and a momo-making session give you a glimpse of village life, before the trek finishes via Haljure and Banau and a drive back to Pokhara through Kushma.

Why choose the Multiple Viewpoints Trek?

Unlike a trek built around one pass or destination, this itinerary is all about variety: different angles on the same ranges, forest trails, alpine terrain, traditional villages and quieter community-based sections. It is especially appealing if you value photography, sunrises and cultural encounters, and want to explore beyond Nepal's most heavily visited corridors.

Alternative 3Api Himal Trek: remote adventure in far-western Nepal

Best for: experienced, adventurous trekkers seeking remote mountain landscapes, few visitors and a less-developed trekking region.

If Manaslu appealed to you for its remoteness and sense of adventure, consider heading much farther west to Api Himal Base Camp, one of Nepal's least-explored Himalayan destinations.

In the far west of the country, around Darchula, the Api Himal area feels very different from the established routes around Pokhara and the Annapurna region. Mountains, isolated settlements, forests, river valleys and limited tourism infrastructure give it a distinctly wild character, and a quieter side of the Himalayas.

What makes Api Himal different?

Api Himal is not a conventional teahouse destination with services at every stop. Depending on the itinerary, you may meet remote trails, basic accommodation, camping requirements and limited access to supplies. That makes planning especially important: transport, food, accommodation, local support and emergency arrangements all need careful thought before departure.

Getting there generally means travelling to far-western Nepal, with the approach depending on your trailhead and current transport. Options include a flight to Dhangadhi followed by a long overland journey, or a longer road trip from elsewhere in Nepal. Confirm the actual approach with a local operator before booking.

With extra time, Api Himal can potentially be combined with Bardia National Park, creating a wider western Nepal itinerary that pairs remote mountains with lowland wildlife. This needs additional travel days and careful logistics, not just a simple extension of the trek.

Why choose Api Himal instead of Manaslu?

Api Himal appeals to travellers who value exploration, solitude and visiting a region outside Nepal's mainstream circuit. Where the Annapurna Circuit has established infrastructure and the Multiple Viewpoints Trek mixes popular highlights with quieter trails, Api Himal is a more remote undertaking that calls for more flexibility, preparation and local expertise.

It isn't necessarily the easiest or quickest replacement for a cancelled Manaslu trip. It is best for trekkers with enough time, comfort with basic facilities and an appetite for a more adventurous expedition.

Plan your next Himalayan adventure

The disruption to the Manaslu Circuit and Tsum Valley is a reminder that mountain plans must stay flexible, particularly after extreme weather. Choosing another destination should never mean compromising on safety or overlooking the latest local conditions, and no departure date is worth trusting a makeshift passage that even locals find hard to cross.

For a well-established high-altitude adventure, the Annapurna Circuit remains a compelling option. For a broader range of Himalayan panoramas, consider the Multiple Viewpoints Trek. And experienced adventurers with more time may find Api Himal Base Camp an exciting way to discover far-western Nepal.
