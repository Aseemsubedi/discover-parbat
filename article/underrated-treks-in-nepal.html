Underrated Treks in Nepal: 6 Quiet Routes Our Local Guides Recommend

Big Himalayan views without the queue at Poon Hill or the crowds on the Annapurna Base Camp trail. These are the treks we send friends on when they ask where Nepal still feels quiet.

The quick answer

The most underrated treks in Nepal, in our experience, are Khopra Ridge, Kokhe Danda, Muldai Viewpoint, Panchase, Pikey Peak and Api Himal Base Camp.

Four of them start from Pokhara and sit in the shadow of famous neighbours: Poon Hill, Ghorepani and Annapurna Base Camp take most of the trekkers, while these ridges a few hours away stay quiet. Pikey Peak gives you Everest views without the Lukla flight, and Api Himal in the far west is about as remote as a teahouse-and-camp trek in Nepal gets.

We are a small local company from Parbat district, and we have walked every one of these routes ourselves. One of them, the Kokhe Danda Trek, is a trail we found in 2020 and opened with our own guides, so we are not neutral about it. We will tell you where each trek shines and where it does not, so you can choose with your eyes open.

Underrated trek 1Khopra Ridge: Dhaulagiri up close, without the base camp crowds

Best for: fit trekkers who want ABC-level mountain scenery on a quieter trail, and anyone who likes a ridge-top lodge with the view right outside the door.

Khopra Ridge, also called Khopra Danda, sits at 3,667 metres on the Annapurna Community Eco-Trail, directly opposite Dhaulagiri, the seventh-highest mountain in the world. The ridge looks across the valley to Dhaulagiri and along the Annapurna range, and the community lodge on top means you wake up to that view rather than climbing to it in the dark with a headtorch.

It is underrated mainly because it sits between two of Nepal's most famous trails. Most trekkers heading this way either turn at Poon Hill or carry on to Annapurna Base Camp, so the forest trail from Tadapani to Dobato and on to Khopra stays quiet even in October. If you have a spare day and good legs, the side trip to the sacred Khayer Lake at 4,653 metres is one of the best day hikes in the region. Our eight-day route also passes Muldai Viewpoint, Poon Hill and Kokhe Danda, so you see the classic viewpoints as well as the quiet ones.

Underrated trek 2Kokhe Danda: the quietest summit near Pokhara, with family homestays

Best for: first-time trekkers, families, and anyone who cares more about village life than ticking off altitude.

The Kokhe Danda Trek climbs to 3,309 metres, the highest point of Parbat district, with views from Dhaulagiri across the Annapurnas to Machhapuchhre. It is underrated for a simple reason: it is new. We found the route in 2020 and built it with our local guides, so it does not appear in older guidebooks, and on most days you will have the summit to yourself.

What makes Kokhe Danda different is where you sleep. Instead of lodges, you stay with families in Haljure and Lespar, Magar villages where people still farm the terraces and cook on the family fire. In Lespar, the evening can end with a Magar cultural dance. The walking days are short, the altitude is gentle, and jeep access at Haljure, Lespar and Phalame Danda makes it easy to shorten the trek if you need to.

The standard trek is five days from Pokhara, with a 3-day short version for a long weekend. For the day-by-day route, map and cost, see the main trek page. If you are comparing it with the classic, read why Kokhe Danda is a quieter alternative to Poon Hill.

Underrated trek 3Muldai Viewpoint: a higher, wider sunrise than Poon Hill

Best for: trekkers who want the Poon Hill experience without sharing the sunrise with hundreds of people.

Muldai Viewpoint, often written Mulde, stands at 3,637 metres, about a 30-minute climb above Dobato. From the top the panorama runs from Dhaulagiri to Manaslu, a wider sweep than Poon Hill and more than 400 metres higher. Yet most trekkers have never heard of it, because it sits off the main Ghorepani–Ghandruk loop.

Our six-day Muldai Viewpoint Trek climbs through rhododendron forest to Dobato, catches sunrise at Muldai, crosses to Ghorepani for Poon Hill, and finishes with Kokhe Danda and a homestay night in Lespar. You get the famous viewpoint and the quiet ones in a single loop, which makes it a good choice if you do not want to miss Poon Hill but do not want it to be the whole trip. Our Muldai vs Poon Hill comparison goes into the details.

Underrated trek 4Panchase: three easy days, Gurung hospitality and a sunrise over Machhapuchhre

Best for: beginners, short trips and anyone who wants a gentle introduction to trekking in Nepal.

Panchase is a forested ridge on the hills west of Pokhara, topping out at about 2,500 metres. It is underrated because it looks small on paper: no famous peak, no base camp, no big altitude number. What it does have is one of the best sunrise views over Dhaulagiri, the Annapurna range and Machhapuchhre that you can reach in under three days, plus orchid forests and very few other walkers.

Our three-day Panchase Trek drives from Pokhara via Kushma to the Gurung village of Dandakharka, spends the first night with a family in Arthar, then climbs to Panchase for sunrise before descending through Bhanjyang to Ghatichhina. Walking days are short, altitude is not a concern for most people, and it works in winter if you bring warm layers for the night on top.

Underrated trek 5Pikey Peak: Everest views without the Lukla flight

Best for: trekkers who want to see Everest but would rather skip the flight, the crowds and the permit paperwork of the Khumbu.

Pikey Peak (4,065 m) sits in the lower Everest region, south of the Khumbu. From the summit on a clear morning you look across to Everest and a long line of eastern Himalayan peaks, and on the way you pass through Sherpa villages and quiet forest. It is underrated because almost everyone who wants Everest views flies to Lukla instead, so Pikey's family-run teahouses rarely feel busy.

Our seven-day Pikey Peak Trek starts at Dhap, crosses Pikey Peak, drops to the monastery village of Junbesi and finishes in Salleri. No trekking permits are needed, which keeps planning simple. We have done it in late December and in early June; October, November and spring give the clearest views. If you are deciding between this and the Khumbu, read Pikey Peak vs Everest View Trek.

Underrated trek 6Api Himal Base Camp: far-west Nepal, almost nobody else

Best for: experienced trekkers who want genuine remoteness and do not mind basic camps.

Api (7,132 m) is the highest mountain in far-western Nepal, and the trek to its base camp in Darchula is one of the least-walked routes we run. The base camp valley is ringed by Api, Nampa, Bobaye and Kaiyokor, and from the camps you can hike to Kalidhunga Lake (4,160 m) or Ringdepani Lake, each about an hour and a half away. From June the pastures turn green under the snow peaks, and from mid-April hundreds of local people camp around base camp and Dhauli Odhar for the yarsa harvest, which gives the valley a small seasonal market.

Be clear about what this trek is. Camps are basic, not Annapurna- or Everest-style teahouses, trekking days are long, and getting there takes time. Our 14-day Api Himal Base Camp Trek includes the journey west plus a Bardiya safari and Lumbini. You need an Api Nampa Conservation Area permit, which we arrange. Go from October to mid-December or late March to June; it is not the right trek from December to mid-March once the snow arrives. Our Api Himal in photos shows what the valley really looks like.

The honest trade-offs of quieter trails

Underrated does not mean better in every way. On the busiest routes there is a teahouse every hour, Wi-Fi in most of them and a steady stream of people on the trail. On these routes there is less of all of that. Rooms are simpler, menus are shorter, and on Kokhe Danda and Api Himal the trail markings are still minimal, so a guide who knows the way is worth much more than on the Poon Hill steps.

Quiet trails also mean fewer people around if something goes wrong. That is why our guides carry a pulse oximeter and a basic first-aid kit, ask every guest each morning how they slept and whether they have a headache or nausea, and check travel insurance before the higher treks. On the Annapurna routes, Khopra Ridge and Muldai need crampons after snowfall, and your guide will tell you before you leave Pokhara whether to bring them.

Permits and guides: since 2023, foreign trekkers need a licensed guide in Nepal's national parks and conservation areas. That covers Khopra Ridge and Muldai (ACAP) and Api Himal (Api Nampa Conservation Area). The standard Kokhe Danda route and the Pikey Peak Trek need no trekking permits. Nepali trekkers do not need ACAP.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most underrated treks in Nepal?

Our local guides' picks are Khopra Ridge (3,667 m) and Muldai Viewpoint (3,637 m) in the Annapurna region, the Kokhe Danda Trek (3,309 m) and the Panchase Trek near Pokhara, the Pikey Peak Trek (4,065 m) in the lower Everest region, and the Api Himal Base Camp Trek in far-western Nepal. All give big Himalayan views with far fewer trekkers than Poon Hill, Annapurna Base Camp or Everest Base Camp.

What is the least crowded trek near Pokhara?

The Kokhe Danda Trek in Parbat district is one of the quietest. It reaches 3,309 m, the highest point of Parbat, in five easy days from Pokhara (three on the short version), with nights in family homestays in Haljure and Lespar rather than busy lodges. Khopra Ridge and Muldai Viewpoint are also much quieter than Poon Hill or Annapurna Base Camp.

Which underrated trek in Nepal is best for beginners?

The Panchase Trek (3 days, about 2,500 m) and the Kokhe Danda Trek (5 days, 3,309 m) are the easiest. Walking days are short, altitude is not a concern for most people, and you stay in village homestays. Both start and finish in Pokhara.

Are less crowded treks in Nepal safe?

Yes, when you go with a local guide who knows the trail. Quieter routes have fewer people around to help, so the guide matters more. Our guides carry a pulse oximeter and a basic first-aid kit, check on every guest each morning, and ask about travel insurance before higher treks.

Do I need a guide for these treks?

Foreign trekkers need a licensed guide in Nepal's national parks and conservation areas, which covers Khopra Ridge, Muldai Viewpoint and Api Himal. On the standard Kokhe Danda route a guide is not enforced, but we still recommend one, because the trail is new, signs are few and the homestay villages are part of the experience.

Can I do these treks in winter?

Kokhe Danda, Panchase, Muldai Viewpoint and Khopra Ridge all work from December to February, though crampons are needed on Muldai and Khopra after snowfall. Pikey Peak is possible but cold at the summit. Api Himal is not recommended from December to mid-March.
